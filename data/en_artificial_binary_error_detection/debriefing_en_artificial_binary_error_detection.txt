We really appreciate your time and your careful attention. Here's what this study was about.

Language researchers often ask native speakers how natural or acceptable a sentence sounds, and use those judgments to understand how language works in the mind. The traditional view is that a sentence sounds worse simply because it breaks a grammatical rule. We're testing a different idea: that a sentence sounds acceptable to the extent that it still gets a clear meaning across.

That's why some of the sentences looked a little off — we created them by swapping neighboring words, once in some sentences and three times in others. About a third of the sentences you saw had nothing changed at all.

Earlier studies have found that the same small change bothers people less in a long sentence than in a short one. One possible reason is simply that an error is harder to spot when there is more sentence around it. That is why we asked you, for each sentence, both whether it contained an error and how acceptable it sounded, and why the sentences came in short, medium and long versions: it lets us see whether a change sounds less bad in a longer sentence even when you noticed it.

If you found yourself unsure on some of the sentences, that is genuinely useful to us — that uncertainty is part of what we are measuring.

Thanks again for helping us learn how people understand language!
