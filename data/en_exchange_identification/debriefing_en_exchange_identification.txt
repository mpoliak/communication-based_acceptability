We really appreciate your time and your careful attention. Here's what this study was about.

Language researchers often ask native speakers how natural or acceptable a sentence sounds, and use those judgments to understand how language works in the mind. The traditional view is that a sentence sounds worse simply because it breaks a grammatical rule. We're testing a different idea: that a sentence sounds acceptable to the extent that it still gets a clear meaning across.

That's why some of the sentences looked a little off — we created them by swapping two neighboring words. About half the sentences you saw were left exactly as they were originally written, which is why clicking nothing was often the right response.

Earlier studies have found that the same small change bothers people less in a long sentence than in a short one. One possible reason is simply that an error is harder to spot when there is more sentence around it. That is what this task was designed to test: instead of asking how a sentence sounds, we asked you to point to what was wrong, so we can see how often a misplaced word gets noticed at all — and whether that depends on how long the sentence is.

If you found yourself unsure on some of the sentences, that is genuinely useful to us — that uncertainty is part of what we are measuring.

Thanks again for helping us learn how people understand language!
